PULSE WAVE / LAST 10 S
GREENVERKRUYSSE 2008

S = G / mean(G) - 1

Blood absorbs green light most, so the green channel alone carries the pulse. Simple, but any change in brightness from motion or lighting looks like a pulse too.

CHROMDE HAAN 2013

X = 3R - 2G, Y = 1.5R + G - 1.5B
S = X - (std X / std Y) Y

Two colour differences that both cancel white light. Subtracting one from the other removes brightness changes and keeps the colour change caused by blood.

POSWANG 2017

S1 = G - B, S2 = G + B - 2R
h = S1 + (std S1 / std S2) S2

Projects the colour onto a plane orthogonal to skin tone, re-tuned every 1.6 s, so the pulse survives slow changes in lighting and skin colour.

HOW WELL IT WORKSSIMULATED

01

Raw colour

g[n] = mean green of the skin in frame n

Thirty times a second the forehead and cheeks are averaged. The pulse is under 1 percent of this signal, buried under slow lighting drift.

02

Detrend CONVOLUTION

d[n] = g[n] - (g * h)[n], h = 2 s moving average

Convolving with a moving average gives the slow trend. Subtracting it removes the drift and leaves the fast part.

03

Bandpass CONVOLUTION

y[n] = sum h[k] d[n - k], pass 0.7 to 4 Hz

A windowed-sinc FIR filter keeps only rhythms a heart can have: 42 to 240 beats per minute.

04

Spectrum FOURIER TRANSFORM

Y(f) = sum y[n] e^(-j 2 pi f n / fs)

The FFT measures how much of each rhythm the signal holds. Green, CHROM and POS each give one spectrum, and TRACE selects the one with the sharpest peak after removing motion frequencies.

05

Heart rate

BPM = 60 x f_peak

The tallest peak is the heart rate. A check at half the frequency stops a strong harmonic being read as double the rate.

Steps 1 to 3 show the green channel, the easiest to follow. CHROM and POS go through the same detrend and bandpass. Start a measurement to see live signals.

A photo or a screen has no blood flow, so it has no pulse.

HOW

Every half second, CHROM and POS must both find a clean peak and agree within 5 BPM. The dial shows how often that held over the last 10 s. Try the simulator's "Photo" switch, or hold up a printed photo.

Heart rhythm

IN THIS APP

READY

The tiny changes between beats reveal stress, rest and breathing.

HOW

Beats are timed from the pulse wave. A second Fourier transform of the gaps between them splits heart-rate variability into LF and HF bands. LF/HF needs at least two minutes of steady video.

HOW

Slow breathing at 6 per minute is known to raise heart-rate variability. Pace while recording the heart rhythm: its beat-to-beat chart should rise and fall with the circle.
