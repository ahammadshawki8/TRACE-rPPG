PULSE WAVE / LAST 10 S
GREENVERKRUYSSE 2008

S = G / mean(G) - 1

Blood absorbs green light most, so the green channel alone carries the pulse. Simple, but any change in brightness from motion or lighting looks like a pulse too.

CHROMDE HAAN 2013

X = 3R - 2G, Y = 1.5R + G - 1.5B
S = X - (std X / std Y) Y

Two colour differences that both cancel white light. Subtracting one from the other removes brightness changes and keeps the colour change caused by blood.

POSWANG 2017

S1 = G - B, S2 = G + B - 2R
h = S1 + (std S1 / std S2) S2

Projects the colour onto a plane orthogonal to skin tone, re-tuned every 1.6 s, so the pulse survives slow changes in lighting and skin colour.

TRACE rPPG

A camera can feel your pulse.

Every heartbeat makes your face a tiny bit redder. Too small for eyes, big enough for maths.

TRACE: Tone-stratified Robustness Across Compressed Encodings. rPPG: remote photoplethysmography.

WHY THIS PROBLEM

Measuring a pulse still needs wires.

Clips, straps and electrodes work, but not when

  • the doctor is on a video call,
  • the patient is a newborn with fragile skin,
  • the person is driving.

In each case a camera is already looking at the face. We wanted to see how far that camera alone can go.

THE IDEA

Blood drinks green light.

Each beat pushes a little more blood into the skin. More blood soaks up more green light, so less bounces back to the camera.

Go deeper

Haemoglobin absorbs green (about 540 nm) most strongly. The camera's green channel therefore carries the strongest pulse, under 1 percent of the pixel value. Melanin absorbs light too, which is why darker skin gives a weaker signal.

STEP 1 / CAPTURE

Grab the colour, 30 times a second.

We find the face, then average the forehead and both cheeks in every frame. One colour per frame becomes a signal over time.

FOURIER SHIFT THEOREM, FOR TRACKING
Go deeper

A Haar detector finds the face once. After that, phase correlation tracks it: a shift in the image is a linear phase in its Fourier transform, so the peak of the inverse FFT gives the movement to a fraction of a pixel.

g[n] = mean green of the skin in frame n

LIVE: RAW GREEN, 10 S
STEP 2 / DETREND

Flatten the slow hills.

Lighting drifts slowly and hides the pulse. We slide a 2 second average along the signal and subtract it: the hills go, the heartbeat ripples stay.

CONVOLUTION
Go deeper

d[n] = g[n] - (g * h)[n], h = 2 s moving average

Convolving with a moving average is a low-pass filter. Subtracting it leaves a high-pass. Its first null sits at 0.49 Hz, safely below the slowest heart rate we look for (0.7 Hz).

LIVE: AFTER DETREND
STEP 3 / BAND-PASS

Only heart speeds get in.

A filter lets through rhythms between 42 and 240 beats per minute. Breathing is too slow, sensor noise is too fast: both are turned away.

CONVOLUTION
Go deeper

y[n] = sum h[k] d[n - k], pass 0.7 to 4 Hz

A windowed-sinc FIR filter, built by hand. It runs forwards and backwards (zero phase) so beats are not delayed, which matters when we time them.

LIVE: AFTER BAND-PASS
STEP 4 / THE THREE METHODS

Meet Green, CHROM and POS.

Each frame gives three numbers: red, green, blue. The three methods are three recipes for mixing them into one pulse signal.

  • Green uses only the green channel. Simple, but anything that changes brightness looks like a pulse.
  • CHROM subtracts two colour differences that both cancel white light, so brightness changes drop out.
  • POS looks along a direction at right angles to your own skin tone, re-aimed every 1.6 seconds.
Go deeper

GREEN S = G / mean(G) - 1

CHROM X = 3R - 2G, Y = 1.5R + G - 1.5B, S = X - (std X / std Y) Y

POS S1 = G - B, S2 = G + B - 2R, h = S1 + (std S1 / std S2) S2

Green is from Verkruysse 2008, CHROM from de Haan 2013, POS from Wang 2017. The mixing weights come from the physics of light reflecting off skin, not from training.

STEP 5 / FIND THE BEAT

A prism for rhythms.

The Fourier transform splits the wiggle into its rhythms, like a prism splits light. The tallest bar is the heartbeat.

FOURIER TRANSFORM FOURIER SERIES
Go deeper

Y(f) = sum y[n] e^(-j 2 pi f n / fs), BPM = 60 x f_peak

A sharp pulse is a Fourier series: a fundamental plus harmonics at 2f, 3f. If the harmonic ever looks taller, we check half the frequency before trusting it, so we never report double the real rate.

STEP 6 / TRACE DECIDES

TRACE picks the sharpest peak.

Every half second each method's spectrum is scored: one clean peak scores high, a mess scores low. Frequencies that match head motion are masked first. The winner gives the heart rate.

Go deeper

Quality = power in the peak and its harmonic divided by all power in the heart band (Parseval: power in the spectrum equals energy in time). A motion reference, blind to blood, marks the frequencies to suppress. On 3,060 simulated readings never used for tuning, selecting per window beat every single method.

CHALLENGE 1 / LIGHTING

Three pairs of glasses.

A lamp flickers, a cloud passes, a screen glows: brightness changes all the time. Green cannot tell that from a pulse and is fooled. CHROM and POS mix colours so that brightness cancels out, and the pulse stays.

Go deeper

GREEN S = G / mean(G) - 1

CHROM X = 3R - 2G, Y = 1.5R + G - 1.5B, S = X - (std X / std Y) Y

POS S1 = G - B, S2 = G + B - 2R, h = S1 + (std S1 / std S2) S2

Both CHROM and POS are fixed projections from the physics of skin reflection, not learned from data.

CHALLENGE 2 / MOTION

Stay on the face, ignore the sway.

First the box follows the face, so we keep reading the same skin. Then a second signal that sees motion but not blood tells TRACE which rhythms are sway, and those are masked out before choosing.

Go deeper

Tracking uses phase correlation: a shift between frames is a linear phase in the Fourier transform, so the peak of the inverse FFT gives the movement to a fraction of a pixel. The motion reference is the brightness direction with the blood direction removed, so it sees shading and sway but not the pulse. Heavy, fast motion is still the hardest case, and the numbers show it.

CHALLENGE 3 / SKIN TONE

Every skin tone, same heartbeat.

Melanin absorbs light too, so on darker skin the pulse is fainter, not gone. We make every tone count the same way:

  • the skin mask learns each person's own skin colour, instead of a fixed colour rule
  • each colour is divided by its own average, so a small change on dark skin weighs as much as a big one on light skin
  • POS aims at each person's own skin tone, and we test on all six skin types
Go deeper

The skin types are the Fitzpatrick scale, I (always burns) to VI (never burns). Darker skin is still harder, and we report that openly: the table shows TRACE's error by skin type on the simulated volunteers, and the Scenarios screen adds real volunteers as they are recorded.

HOW WE KNOW

Proof from three sources.

Every decision TRACE makes is checked against three kinds of truth: simulated faces where the answer is exact, public recordings of people sitting still and talking with a finger or wrist sensor, and our own volunteers with a smartwatch.

WHAT'S NEXT

More than a heart rate.

A camera that can feel a pulse can also tell a real face from a photo, read heart rhythm and stress, and coach slow breathing. Further out: telehealth check-ins, driver fatigue, newborn and elder care.

THE COURSE, IN ONE PICTURE

Where our syllabus lives.

  • CONVOLUTION removing drift, and the band-pass filter
  • FOURIER TRANSFORM the spectrum and the heart rate, face tracking, and heart rhythm (used twice)
  • FOURIER SERIES harmonics, so we never report double

    PROTOTYPE

    START A MEASUREMENT

    A photo or a screen has no blood flow, so it has no pulse.

    HOW

    Every half second, CHROM and POS must both find a clean peak and agree within 5 BPM. The dial shows how often that held over the last 10 s. Try the simulator's "Photo" switch, or hold up a printed photo.

    02

    Heart rhythm

    IN THIS APP
    0:00/ 2:00
    READY

    The tiny changes between beats reveal stress, rest and breathing.

    HOW

    Beats are timed from the pulse wave. A second Fourier transform of the gaps between them splits heart-rate variability into LF and HF bands. LF/HF needs at least two minutes of steady video.

    03

    Guided breathing

    IN THIS APP
    6 / MIN
    OFF

    Breathe with the circle, and watch your heart rate follow each breath.

    HOW

    Slow breathing at 6 per minute is known to raise heart-rate variability. Pace while recording the heart rhythm: its beat-to-beat chart should rise and fall with the circle.
