01 · The problem

Every vital sign measurement today needs something touching you

Telehealth
The doctor cannot see your pulse

A video consultation gives the clinician everything except objective physiological data.

Neonatal care
Adhesive sensors damage fragile skin

Premature infants need constant monitoring and can least tolerate contact sensors.

Driver monitoring
Nobody wears a chest strap to drive

Drowsiness and cardiac events are detectable, but only if measurement is invisible.

In all three cases there is already a camera pointed at the person.

02 · The idea

A camera can already see your heartbeat.
It just needs the right mathematics.

every heartbeat pushes blood into the face ↓ the skin absorbs slightly more light ↓ the camera records a tiny colour change ↓ Fourier finds the rhythm in it

The colour change is under one percent. Invisible to the eye. Well within the reach of an ordinary webcam and the three tools this course teaches.

03 · The physics

Why green carries the signal

Haemoglobin
Absorbs green light strongly, red light weakly

So when blood volume rises, the green channel dips much harder than the red channel does. The pulse lives in the difference between channels, not in overall brightness.

Consequence
Motion and lighting look different from a pulse

A person shifting, or a cloud passing, brightens all three channels together. A heartbeat changes their balance. That distinction is what makes the whole thing possible.

This one fact is what we exploit at every later stage: artifacts change brightness, blood changes colour balance.

05 · Course topic 1

Convolution — twice, and we prove the theorem

Step 4
Detrending

Convolve with a moving-average kernel to extract the slow lighting drift, then subtract it. A low-pass subtracted from the original is a high-pass.

Step 6
Bandpass filtering

Convolve with a kernel that keeps only 0.7 to 4 Hz — that is 42 to 240 BPM, the range a human heart rate can plausibly occupy.

and we demonstrate the convolution theorem directly: conv(x, h) ≡ IFFT( FFT(x) × FFT(h) ) identical to 1e-16 — floating point noise, not disagreement
06 · Course topic 2

Fourier Transform — this is where the number comes from

We wind the filtered signal around a circle at every candidate rate. Noise smears evenly and cancels. The real pulse piles up on one side.

BPM = peak frequency × 60
The noise cancelling itself out is not luck. It is the mechanism. That is why we can extract a signal weaker than the noise sitting on top of it.
07 · Course topic 3

Fourier Series — why the spectrum has two peaks

  • A heartbeat is not a sine wave. It is a sharp upstroke, a fast fall, a small secondary bump.Fourier series says a sharp repeating shape must be built from a fundamental plus harmonics.
  • So the spectrum shows a peak at 72 BPM and another at 144 BPM.The second is the harmonic. It is not a second heartbeat.
  • Naive peak-picking will sometimes choose the wrong one.If the fundamental is weakened by filtering or poor light, argmax reports 144 for a resting adult.
We handle this explicitly with a sub-harmonic check — before accepting a peak at f, we test f/2. This is a known failure mode we designed against, and it comes straight out of Fourier series theory.
08 · Our contribution

Existing systems pick one method and hope

Method A
Green channel

Simple. Survives compression reasonably. Collapses under motion.

Method B
CHROM

Motion-robust projection. Needs decent lighting and intact colour detail.

Method C
POS

Best in clean conditions. Also depends on colour fidelity.

Each one wins under different conditions. Every published implementation commits to one for the entire recording. That is a bet, not a measurement.
09 · Our contribution

TRACE: measure how much to trust each method, every frame

Run all three in parallel. Score each one live. Blend their spectra weighted by those scores.

  • Lighting drops → green is down-weighted
  • Subject moves → green collapses, CHROM takes over
  • No single method is trusted blindly
10 · The quality metric

And the trust score comes straight out of the FFT

peak = argmax( power inside 0.7–4 Hz ) signalPower = power near the peak + power near its harmonic noisePower = everything else in the band quality = signalPower / (signalPower + noisePower) → 0 to 1
  • A clean pulse concentrates its energy into one narrow peak. Score near 1.
  • Noise spreads energy evenly across the band. Score near 0.
  • No extra computation. It is a ratio of sums over the spectrum we already computed. Parseval's theorem is what lets us measure energy this way.
11 · Extension one

Testing it on the video a real telehealth call produces

Every benchmark dataset is clean, uncompressed video. Real usage is Zoom.

  • Codecs store colour at a quarter resolution because human eyes barely notice
  • But colour is exactly where our signal lives
  • So we route footage through real calls and measure the damage
We predict the ranking of methods flips at low bitrate — and that is precisely the condition adaptive fusion exists for.
12 · Extension two

Applying the Fourier Transform a second time

first transform — on the video signal brightness over time → FFT → heart rate second transform — on the rhythm of the beats themselves gaps between beats → FFT → autonomic state
  • LF band, 0.04–0.15 Hz. Mixed autonomic activity and blood-pressure regulation.
  • HF band, 0.15–0.40 Hz. That is 9 to 24 breaths per minute — this peak is the person's breathing, visible inside their heartbeat.
  • Output is a wellness indicator, never a diagnosis. We state that explicitly on screen and in the report.

14 · Proving it works

Quantified validation, not a number that looks about right

Ground truth
Public benchmark datasets

UBFC-rPPG — 42 videos with synchronised pulse-oximeter readings, free to download. PURE — six controlled head-motion scenarios. Plus our own recordings with a pulse oximeter.

Metrics
What every paper in this field reports

MAE, RMSE and Pearson r, plus Bland-Altman analysis to expose systematic bias that an average error hides completely.

The key test
Ablation study

Each method alone, then fusion. This is what turns "our method is better" from a claim into evidence.
