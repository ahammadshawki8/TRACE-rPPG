The two kernels, and what they do to your frequencies

Both filters are convolutions. A kernel on its own tells you very little; its frequency response tells you everything.

Kernel A

Moving average

—
Used for detrending. The first null is a frequency this filter deletes completely — keep it far below the heart-rate band, or subtracting the trend would remove the pulse itself.
Kernel B

Windowed-sinc bandpass

—
Built by hand from a truncated sinc, not pulled from a library. The ripples in the stopband are the price of truncation — the same leakage trade-off as windowing your data.

The winding machine, run

The filtered signal wound around a circle at every candidate rate. Where the dots pile up is the heart rate; how much of the band's energy sits in that pile is the quality score.

The twelve checks, drawn

Each panel is one assertion from scripts/step1_synthetic.py, plotted rather than printed.

Check 3

Convolution theorem

—
Sliding a kernel along the signal and multiplying two spectra give identical numbers. The two traces sit exactly on top of each other; the residual is floating-point rounding, not disagreement.
Check 4

The wraparound trap

—
Filtering through the FFT without zero-padding makes the signal loop, so its tail contaminates its head. The damage is confined to exactly the first len(h)−1 samples, and it is not subtle.
Check 6

The harmonic trap

—
A pulse whose fundamental has been suppressed. Naive argmax takes the tallest spike and reports double the true rate. The sub-harmonic check looks at half the peak frequency and recovers it — while never firing on a healthy reading from 86 to 140 BPM.
Check 7

Frequency resolution

two rhythms, 72 and 78 BPM
Identical signal, three window lengths. Resolution is 1/T, so separating rhythms 6 BPM apart needs more than 10 seconds. At 6 s they fuse into one hump and you would confidently report a single wrong rate.
Check 8

Parabolic interpolation

—
The true rate sits between two bins. Fitting a parabola through the peak and its two neighbours finds the vertex, cutting mean error from 0.420 to 0.022 BPM across five off-grid rates — for three lines of code.
Check 9

Noise robustness

the pulse survives well below the noise
At −6 dB the noise carries four times the power of the pulse and the trace looks like nothing. The estimate is still within 0.3 BPM, because noise is not rhythmic and cancels when wound around a circle, while the pulse accumulates.
